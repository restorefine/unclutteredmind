Part 1

The product

The brief

The problem. With ADHD, the hard part usually isn't knowing what to do. It's holding it all in your head, starting, judging how long things take, and switching between tasks. Ordinary to-do apps make things worse: long lists, lots of fields to fill in, and red overdue badges that pile up.

What Uncluttered Mind does instead. You get everything out of your head in one go, by voice or typing, in the app or on WhatsApp. The app works out what each task is, when it should happen and how long it will take. It lays the tasks into your free time with a breather between each one. Then it hides the list and shows only what matters right now, with a bar that makes time visible. WhatsApp gives a nudge before, at the start and at the end of each task, with buttons to tap so you never have to open the app.

The design rules behind every feature:

  • One thing at a time. The focus card shows only the current or next task.
  • Getting things in must be effortless. No forms, no fields. Just talk.
  • Make starting easy. "Start it now", tiny first steps, "just do the first 2 minutes".
  • Make time visible. Time bars, heads-ups and "5 minutes left" warnings.
  • No guilt. "Not today" is always one tap away. Missed tasks get a gentle check, not a red badge. Everything can be undone.
  • It adapts to the day you're having. Energy check-ins and a "Too much?" button.

How a day works

  1. Brain dumpTalk or type in the app, or text or voice-note the business number.
  2. UnderstandAI (or the built-in rules) splits it into tasks with times, lengths and repeats.
  3. CheckA preview shows what it heard. Fix a title or remove a line, then tap "Add".
  4. PlanSet times stay put. Everything else goes into free time with breathers.
  5. DoThe focus card and WhatsApp nudges guide each task. Tap Done, +15 min or Tomorrow.

Every morning at 8:00 a WhatsApp message lists the day and asks about your energy. On Sunday evening there's a short recap of what got done.

Features

Each feature shows what you see, followed by how it works so you both know what to expect.

Brain dump: voice, typing or WhatsApp

Capture friction

What you see

A big "What's on your mind?" box with a mic button. Say everything in one go, e.g. "call the bank at 2, gym after work, buy milk and eggs, finish the report by 5, urgent". A preview lists the tasks it found, each with an editable title and a time label, then "Add to my day".

How it works

  • With an AI key, the server asks the AI to read the dump. If that fails, it falls back to the built-in parser automatically.
  • The mic uses the browser's speech recognition (Chrome, Edge, Safari). Elsewhere it records audio and the server transcribes it (needs OPENAI_API_KEY).
  • A text or voice note sent to the business number goes through the same process, then the bot replies with what it added.

Automatic planning with breathers

Overwhelm, transitions

What you see

The tasks appear on a timeline for the day, with a gap between each one, and long gaps are labelled "1 hr free". A bar shows how full the day is and warns "That's a lot for one day" at 80%. Anything that won't fit goes into "No room for these", with Fit in today, Tomorrow and Someday buttons.

How it works

  • Tasks with a set time ("at 2") stay exactly where they are.
  • Flexible tasks go into the first free slot, within any window that was said ("by 5", "after work"). Urgent tasks are placed first.
  • The day runs 8am–10pm with a 10-minute breather by default; both can be changed in Settings.
  • Lengths are estimated when not said: calls and emails 15 min, meds 5, gym and meetings 1 hour, anything else 30.

The focus card

One thing at a time

What you see

One big card at the top showing Right now (minutes left and a time bar) or Up next (with a "Start it now" button). If a task ended without being ticked, the card asks "Did you finish it?" with Yes, Do it next and Tomorrow. Its buttons are Done, +15 min, Not today and, with AI, I'm stuck.

How it works

  • "Start it now" moves the next task to the current time.
  • +15 min pushes the task back, and rolls it to tomorrow if there's no room left today.
  • The missed-task check covers tasks that ended in the last 3 hours.
  • Ticking a task off shows a short message and an Undo button for 6 seconds.

Small steps ("I'm stuck")

Getting started

What you see

Tap "I'm stuck" or "Break it into small steps" and a vague task like "Sort my taxes" becomes 3–6 tiny steps. The first step always takes under 2 minutes, e.g. "Open the laptop and find last year's P60". The focus card shows "Step 1 of 4" with a Step done button. You can also add steps yourself.

How it works

  • The AI is asked for concrete, physical steps. The first one must be tiny.
  • Ticking the last step completes the task automatically.
  • The WhatsApp start message includes the first unfinished step.
  • Texting STUCK breaks down the current task on WhatsApp.

WhatsApp nudges

Time blindness, out of sight

For a 1-hour task at 2:00pm, with the default settings:

  1. 1:50pm
    Heads-up"Heads up: Essay in 10 min. Finish what you're doing and grab some water."
  2. 2:00pm
    Start, with buttonsShows the first step (or "Just do the first 2 minutes") with buttons: Done · +15 min · Tomorrow.
  3. 2:05pm
    Gentle second nudgeOnly if there's been no reply: "Still with me? Even 2 minutes counts."
  4. 2:55pm
    5 minutes leftOnly on tasks of 45 min or longer. It names what's next.
  5. 3:00pm
    Check-in"Did you finish Essay?" with the same three buttons.

Also sent

  • Morning plan at 8:00, asking "How's your energy today?" with Low · OK · High buttons.
  • Sunday recap at 7pm: how many things got done, with no streaks or scores.
  • Quiet hours, 10:30pm–7am by default: no messages except for urgent tasks.

The 24-hour rule

WhatsApp only lets a business send normal messages and buttons within 24 hours of the person's last message to it. After that, only pre-approved templates are delivered. The app handles this automatically. Texting the bot once a day (e.g. TODAY) keeps the buttons working.

"Too much? Keep my top 3"

Overwhelm

What you see

A button under the day bar whenever more than 3 flexible tasks are left. Tap it and the day shrinks right away, with a "Breathe" message and an Undo button. On WhatsApp, text TOO MUCH (or "overwhelmed", "help me").

How it works

It keeps everything with a set time, the task in progress, and the top 3 flexible tasks (urgent first, then earliest). Everything else moves to tomorrow and gets a new slot there. Undo moves them back.

Energy check-in

Bad days, good days

What you see

"How's your energy today?" appears in the app (when the day has tasks) and in the morning WhatsApp message. Tap Low, OK or High.

How it works

  • Low: keeps the 3 easiest tasks and moves the rest to tomorrow (Undo available).
  • High: re-orders the day so the most demanding task comes first.
  • OK: no change.
  • Tasks are marked easy or demanding automatically ("essay", "report" and "taxes" count as demanding).

Repeats and the someday list

Routines, ideas with no date

What you see

Say "meds every morning at 8", "bins every Tuesday night" or "gym on Mondays and Thursdays at 6" and they repeat automatically. Settings → Repeats lists them, each with a Stop repeating button. Say "learn Spanish someday" and it goes on the Someday list, with a Do today button, instead of cluttering today.

How it works

  • Repeat copies are created for today and the next 7 days, and topped up daily.
  • If today's time has already passed, today's copy is skipped.
  • Deleting one copy doesn't bring it back; Stop repeating removes future copies.

Works without the server, too

Resilience

What you see

If the page is opened without the server (for example as a preview), it shows "This device only" with an example day, and everything except WhatsApp, voice notes and AI still works.

How it works

The parser and planner live in one shared file (public/shared.js) that runs in both the browser and the server. That means behaviour is identical in both, and in this mode the browser keeps the data in its own storage.

Part 2

Hosting

What you're deploying

One Node.js process with no npm dependencies. It serves the app, runs a login-protected JSON API (password or Face ID / Touch ID passkey), receives WhatsApp events on /webhook, checks for due reminders every 30 seconds, and stores everything in a SQLite database built into Node (transactional writes, 14 daily backups).

lib/reminders.jsPure function deciding which reminders are due (fully unit-tested)

Persistent diskMount it at /data and set DATA_DIR. Without it, every deploy wipes the data.

Hosting steps

First, run the tests locally. They should print pass 36, fail 0.

unzip unclutteredmind.zip && cd unclutteredmind
npm test
cp .env.example .env    # set APP_USERNAME and APP_PASSWORD
npm start               # http://localhost:3000

Option A: VPS with Docker Compose and Caddy Recommended

Any small Ubuntu/Debian VPS (1 vCPU, 512 MB is plenty). Caddy handles HTTPS certificates automatically.

  1. Point DNS. Create an A record for the subdomain (e.g. app.unclutteredmind.com) pointing at the VPS IP.
  2. Install Docker and open the firewall.
    curl -fsSL https://get.docker.com | sh
ufw allow OpenSSH && ufw allow 80 && ufw allow 443 && ufw enable
  3. Upload and configure. Set at least DOMAIN, APP_USERNAME, APP_PASSWORD, APP_ORIGIN and TZ. Compose already sets DATA_DIR=/data and TRUST_PROXY=1.
    unzip unclutteredmind.zip && cd unclutteredmind
cp .env.example .env && nano .env
  4. Start it.
    docker compose up -d --build
docker compose logs -f app
  5. Check. https://<domain>/healthz returns {"ok":true,…} and the site shows the sign-in screen. After the first password sign-in it offers to set up Face ID / Touch ID. To update later: upload the new code and run docker compose up -d --build. Data is kept in the um-data volume.
TestedThe Docker image has been built and run: the health check passes, tasks survive a container restart, and the daily backup is written.

Option B: Render, Railway or Fly.io

  1. Push the folder to a private GitHub repo and create a web service from the Dockerfile.
  2. Add a persistent disk mounted at /data (1 GB).
  3. Set instances to 1, turn off autoscaling, and choose a plan that never sleeps.
  4. Set APP_USERNAME, APP_PASSWORD, APP_ORIGIN, TZ=Europe/London, DATA_DIR=/data, TRUST_PROXY=1 and the WhatsApp/AI variables.
  5. Set the health check path to /healthz and add the custom domain.

Option C: plain Node on an existing server

Run node server.js under systemd or pm2, behind Nginx or Caddy for HTTPS, with TRUST_PROXY=1.

[Unit]
Description=Uncluttered Mind
After=network.target

[Service]

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target

WhatsApp setup

Do this once the site is live on HTTPS, because Meta has to reach /webhook.

  1. Create the Meta app. developers.facebook.com → My Apps → Create app → type Business, linked to RestoRefine's Meta Business account. Add the WhatsApp product.
  2. Add the business number. WhatsApp → API Setup → Add phone number, then verify it. Copy the Phone number ID into WA_PHONE_NUMBER_ID. Set up business verification and a payment method in Business Settings so the messaging limits aren't tiny.
  3. Make a permanent token. business.facebook.com → Settings → Users → System users → Add (Admin). Assign the app and the WhatsApp account with full control. Generate a token with whatsapp_business_messaging and whatsapp_business_management, set to never expire, and put it in WA_TOKEN. The 24-hour token on the API Setup page won't do.
  4. Connect the webhook. WhatsApp → Configuration → Webhook → Edit. Callback URL https://<domain>/webhook, verify token = WA_VERIFY_TOKEN. Click Verify and save, then subscribe to the messages field. Put App settings → Basic → App secret into WA_APP_SECRET, restart the app, and set the Meta app to Live.
  5. Set the recipient. Rohit's personal number goes in WA_TO.
  6. Create the templates in WhatsApp Manager → Message templates. Both are Utility, English (UK), body Hi, it's Uncluttered Mind. {{1}} Reply here any time., sample Time for: Call the bank. 15 min, until 2:15pm.
    TemplateNeededButtonsVariable
    unclutteredmind_reminderYesNoneWA_TEMPLATE_NAME
    unclutteredmind_taskRecommendedQuick reply, in this order: Done, +15 min, TomorrowWA_TEMPLATE_BUTTONS_NAME
  7. Test it. Restart the app and check the logs show no WhatsApp warnings. Text hi from Rohit's phone and the help message should come back. Then press Settings → Send a test WhatsApp in the app.
Check with Rohit before moving the numberIf the business number is currently used in the WhatsApp Business app on a phone, Meta's onboarding may move it to the API, and it would then stop working in the phone app. Read Meta's screen carefully. A dedicated number for the bot avoids the issue.
CostsMeta charges for template messages; check its current WhatsApp pricing. Messages sent inside the 24-hour window are replies to the user and work differently.

Go-live checklist

0 of 12 done. Ticks are saved in this browser only.

Running it

  • Logs: one JSON line per event (docker compose logs -f app). Watch for whatsapp send failed (it includes Meta's error code and a hint), WhatsApp could not deliver a message and webhook signature mismatch.
  • Backups: written daily to DATA_DIR/backups/, 14 kept. Copy them off the server daily, e.g. docker compose cp app:/data/backups ./backups-$(date +%F) from cron.
  • Restore: stop the app, delete unclutteredmind.db-wal / -shm if present, copy a backup over DATA_DIR/unclutteredmind.db, then start it. If the database can't be opened, the app logs an error and exits rather than starting empty.
  • Updates: replace the code, run npm test, then redeploy.
  • Security built in:
    • Login cookie: HttpOnly, SameSite, Secure, signed with HMAC, valid for 90 days.
    • Username + password, then Face ID / Touch ID / fingerprint passkeys (only the public key is stored on the server).
    • Lockout after 5 wrong tries; the app won't start in production without a login.
    • A CSRF header is required on every write.
    • Strict Content-Security-Policy.
    • Webhook signatures are checked.
    • Only WA_TO can command the bot, and duplicate deliveries are ignored.
    Keep .env and backups private, since task text can be personal.

Limits and next steps

  • Single user: one login, one WhatsApp recipient. Opening it to other people needs sign-up, per-user data, per-user WhatsApp opt-in, billing and a privacy policy. Treat that as a separate project.
  • No phone push notifications outside WhatsApp yet. Web push would need a service worker and VAPID keys.
  • No Google or Outlook calendar sync yet. Shift patterns have to be entered as repeats.
  • One timezone per deployment (TZ).
